# Marketing launch and learning

This reference plans distribution; it does not operate production releases.
Confirm what is actually available and the audience action the launch supports.
Do not promise unreleased features or repeat testimonials without evidence/permission.

Choose channels based on where the intended audience is observed, not a mandatory
launch-site checklist. Consider owned surfaces, third-party platforms and relevant
partners; a product does not need all three. Explain channel fit and costs.

Prepare the smallest coherent launch package: core message, working destination,
demo or evidence, channel-specific drafts and a follow-up learning plan. Distinguish
prepared, approved, published and verified states. Recheck the destination and exact
draft immediately before any separately authorized publication.

Define the meaningful outcome (for example completed first workflow, qualified
conversation or repeat use), observation window and a failure/stop condition.
Use existing measurement before adding tracking. For low-traffic products, use
direct feedback and task observations rather than underpowered A/B-test claims.
Traffic and reactions do not establish retention or revenue causation.

Keep spend caps, outreach recipients and scheduling approvals explicit. Do not
add popups, waitlists, discounts, tracking or recurring jobs merely to fill a plan.
After an authorized campaign, compare actual observations with the hypothesis;
recommend continue, adjust or stop without retroactively inventing success criteria.
