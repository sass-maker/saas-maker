# Positioning from evidence

Read the product's purpose and current capabilities before its aspirational
roadmap. If evidence is thin, draft a hypothesis rather than declaring an ICP.

Capture only what changes the current decision:

- Who encounters the problem, in what situation, and who decides to pay/use it?
- What do they do today, including doing nothing? What triggers a switch?
- What specific outcome does this product enable, by what mechanism?
- Which objection or switching cost remains? Who is a poor fit?
- What observed capability, customer statement or result supports each claim?

Produce a short positioning statement plus a claim/evidence/gap table when
several claims need comparison. Distinguish customer language from proposed
copy. A repository can prove functionality; it cannot prove customer demand.

Reuse an existing project marketing document if available. Otherwise keep a
one-off brief in the deliverable; create a maintained document only when the
task needs it. Link to PRODUCT.md instead of copying its full contents. Date
material assumptions and propose corrections to the canonical source explicitly.
