# Customer research

Choose the smallest useful mode: analyze supplied material, inspect relevant
public discussions, or prepare interviews/surveys. Running outreach or accessing
private services requires the task's authorization; preparing questions does not.

For each usable observation record a source/date, audience/context, short exact
quote or faithful paraphrase, and what it does and does not establish. Keep
identifying/private material out of public deliverables. Separate independent
participants from repeated posts and distinguish purchasers from noncustomers.

Look for the triggering situation, attempted alternatives, constraints, objection,
desired outcome and language used. Include contradictory evidence and people who
did not convert. Frequency in a selected sample is not population prevalence;
one vocal complaint is not product-market fit or proof of broad demand.

For interviews, ask about a recent actual incident and choices made, not whether
the person likes a proposed feature. Avoid leading questions and hypothetical
willingness-to-pay claims. A proposed interview script is not completed research.

Deliver a compact evidence synthesis: observed pattern, supporting observations,
counterevidence, confidence limits and the next product/message hypothesis to test.
If no relevant evidence is accessible, report that gap rather than fabricate personas.
