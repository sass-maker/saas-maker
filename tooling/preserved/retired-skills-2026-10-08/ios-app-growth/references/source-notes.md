## Thesis to retain

The article describes an iOS subscription-app system in which product
positioning, the App Store page, onboarding, paywall, measurement, creative, and
paid acquisition reinforce one another. The claimed outcome is about $11,000 in
monthly realized revenue after roughly two months, with roughly 30% profit from
paid ads. Those figures are self-reported anecdotes, not benchmarks or promises.

## Directional hypotheses

- Start with a painful or valuable human problem and sell the outcome rather
  than a list of features.
- Narrowing an MVP to native iOS can improve focus for a small team.
- Ship a reviewable, non-risky MVP early and iterate.
- Treat the App Store page, onboarding, and paywall as measurable conversion
  stages.
- Remote paywalls and subscription tooling can make pricing and presentation
  experiments faster.
- A mobile measurement partner can connect app events and subscription revenue
  with ad-platform optimization.
- Apple Ads can capture high-intent search demand; paid social can scale through
  creative and algorithmic learning.
- Test multiple creative formats, stop severe losers, and scale winners in
  bounded steps.
- Paid growth requires cash reserves because ad bills and store payouts have
  different timing.

## Numbers and tactics that require validation

Do not repeat these as facts without current product or first-party evidence:

- iOS share of app purchases or United States revenue;
- specific weekly, monthly, or yearly prices;
- hard paywalls as a default;
- a target onboarding length;
- onboarding completion or download-to-paid benchmarks;
- a fixed per-creative test spend;
- a fixed starting daily budget or automatic budget multiplier;
- a presumed $20-$30 LTV;
- a universal 20-30% campaign scaling rule;
- Apple Ads credits, availability, product names, or campaign options;
- claims that a particular network has the most sophisticated algorithm;
- review-duration expectations or guaranteed approval timelines;
- expected VAT, store fees, vendor fees, or profit-margin ceilings.

## Tactics not carried into the workflow

- Suppressing legitimate negative comments or filtering broad criticism to
  protect ROAS.
- Copying competitor products, onboarding, or viral creatives 1:1.
- Making cancellation harder or inserting ambiguous save/exit offers.
- Treating vulnerable human desires as pressure points.
- Describing ad returns as guaranteed, stock-like, or passive.
- Resetting ratings to conceal a persistent product-quality issue.

The skill retains the article's experimental mindset while replacing its
absolutist claims with explicit measurement, compliance, and loss controls.
