# Fleet public-product monetization options

Date: 2026-09-27. Status: working hypotheses for owner review, not approved pricing or a billing implementation plan.

## Scope and decision rule

The canonical catalog currently has 55 `active` or `primary` identities, of which 42 have `purpose.audience = public` and 13 are internal. This pass covers only the 42 current public identities, including the personal website and DaddyRad umbrella.

Every public identity gets a possible way it could produce money, but **a monetization option is not evidence of a market**. First, qualified people must find the product and complete its core task. Only then is there a reason to test willingness to pay. Some are distribution surfaces for another paid offer; charging each URL separately would double-charge the same audience. A proposed price is a starting experiment, not a revenue forecast. No traffic, conversion, cost, or willingness-to-pay data was supplied for this pass. The parallel audience work must supply those inputs before a charge decision.

Timing means **the first suitable test**, not a promised launch date. The gates apply in order to every row: qualified arrival → successful core use → repeat use or repeated need → expressed willingness to pay → checkout test. A small number of highly qualified buyers may be enough for a specialist tool; ads need much larger volume.

- **Now:** only an existing paid offer can be observed. A pricing conversation with an actual user can begin, but it does not justify building checkout.
- **Next:** test an offer only after the named product gate **and** qualified inbound use are observed. This is conditional, not a 30–90 day promise.
- **Later:** preserve the option, but do not build billing or ads until people arrive, use the product, and the named evidence exists.

All amounts below are USD. For consumer products, local currency, taxes, processor fees, and store fees need a separate checkout decision. Ads use a **$3 page RPM scenario** solely to illustrate scale: 50,000 monthly pageviews would be about $150/month before costs. Actual RPM depends on audience, geography, format, and demand. At small traffic levels, ads should usually wait. Direct sponsorship quotes are test asks, not expected revenue.

## First decisions to make

1. Use the other agent's audience evidence to identify where **qualified people actually arrive**. For each product, record visits or installs, completed core tasks, repeat users, and inbound buying questions. Do not infer buyers from pageviews alone.
2. Use RolePatch as the existing pay-per-use baseline. Verify payment completion, unit cost, and repeat purchase before changing its published packs. A price page alone does not establish demand.
3. Once real users complete workflows, test willingness to pay for CodeVetter, MentionPilot, App Health, and the four public Daddy apps. These have the clearest software value proposition, though the quoted prices remain hypotheses.
4. Feed qualified traffic and retention evidence into the ad and subscription rows. At $3 page RPM, even 50,000 monthly pageviews only models $150/month, so ads should not drive feature priorities without actual yield.
5. Keep the Hub and DaddyRad as bundles/distribution surfaces. Avoid counting the same customer twice across an umbrella and its apps.
6. Hold rights-dependent experiments (Nomad Atlas, Reddit Insights, song worlds, Meme Lab, Mashup) at the validation gate before taking payment or showing ads.
